# Rights Restoration Audit

## Purpose

This working audit routes every selection omitted from the First Field Edition toward the most defensible restoration path. It is an editorial and research tool, not a legal opinion or a finding that any particular use is fair. Fair use must be evaluated selection by selection under 17 U.S.C. §107.

The audit began with 83 omitted selections. After restoring WR-W-046 and substituting a public-domain translation for WR-B-053, the current edition contains 81: 45 copyright-only, 22 rights-plus-source, 11 translation, and 3 source-only cases. The matrix retains cleared entries as restoration history.

## Decision rules

1. Restore verified public-domain text when a reliable witness exists.
2. For source-only cases, authenticate the originating text before making any rights decision.
3. For translations, identify both the underlying work and the particular translation; prefer a verified public-domain translation when editorially suitable.
4. Seek written, limited-print permission for complete poems, song lyrics, complete short works, and substantial creative passages.
5. Reserve fair-use review for limited excerpts serving a documented teaching, criticism, commentary, scholarship, or research purpose. Nonprofit and restricted circulation support but do not decide the analysis.
6. Keep the current omission in place until the restoration route is completed and documented.

## Permission request assumptions

Requests should specify: nonprofit PLC faculty use; no sale or fee; exact passage; number of copies; print-only or separately negotiated digital use; territory; edition; attribution; and requested term. A permission grant should be saved with the entry's research record.

## Review states

Each entry should advance through: **Queued → Source verified → Rights analyzed → Permission requested (if needed) → Cleared or retained as omitted → Text verified → Edition rebuilt and proofed**.

## Initial priority

1. Work the source-only group first; these may yield restorations without a permission campaign.
2. Review translation cases for suitable public-domain witnesses.
3. Group known rights holders by publisher or estate and request narrowly scoped print permissions in batches.
4. Evaluate remaining excerpt cases individually for teaching purpose, amount, creative character, and market substitution.
